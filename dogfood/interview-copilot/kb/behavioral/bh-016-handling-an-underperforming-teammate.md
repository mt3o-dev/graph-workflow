A teammate on our order-management squad had been consistently missing sprint commitments for about six weeks, and other engineers had started quietly picking up his tickets to avoid slippage, which wasn't sustainable and wasn't fair to anyone. I wasn't his manager, but I cared about the team's health, so I asked him to grab coffee outside of any formal review context, deliberately low-stakes, and just asked how things were going. It turned out he was struggling with our codebase's undocumented conventions around our event-sourcing pattern and had been too embarrassed to keep asking basic questions after his first few weeks. I offered to pair with him twice a week specifically on the parts of the codebase he found confusing, not as a performance intervention but as knowledge-sharing, and I also flagged to our manager, with his knowledge, that our onboarding docs for that pattern were genuinely inadequate, which was true regardless of his situation. Within a month his velocity was back to normal, and we ended up rewriting the onboarding guide together, which helped the next two new hires too. I've learned that underperformance is often a symptom of an unaddressed blocker, not a motivation problem, and it's worth investigating before escalating.
