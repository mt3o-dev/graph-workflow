In five years, I want to be operating as a staff-level engineer who owns technical direction across a broader surface than a single service, someone teams come to when a decision has architecture-wide consequences, the way our staff engineers do now for payment infrastructure decisions. I'm deliberately aiming to stay on the technical track rather than move into people management, because what energizes me is deep problem-solving and system design, but I do want to keep growing my ability to influence and mentor across teams, which is a skill I've been actively building through things like the shared retry library I built and got multiple teams to adopt without having formal authority over them. Concretely, I'd like to have led at least one significant system migration or rebuild end to end, been the technical reference for a domain like payments or fraud that other engineers rely on, and mentored a handful of engineers who've grown into strong senior contributors themselves. I care less about a specific title and more about the scope of impact and trust, and I think that comes from consistently solving hard problems well and helping others do the same, which is the trajectory I'm on.
