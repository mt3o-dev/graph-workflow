I estimated a PCI compliance update to our card-tokenization flow at two weeks, based on similar-sounding work I'd done before, but I hadn't accounted for the fact that this particular change required re-certifying with our external PCI auditor, which involved a documentation and evidence-gathering process entirely outside engineering. By the end of week two we were maybe seventy percent done with the code but hadn't even started the audit paperwork, and I realized the real timeline was closer to five weeks. Rather than quietly grinding to make up time or waiting until the original deadline to explain the miss, I flagged it the moment I understood the scope gap, about ten days in, with a clear breakdown of what was code work versus audit process, and a revised estimate with buffer this time. My manager was able to renegotiate the external commitment tied to that date because we gave enough lead time. Since then, I build a standard checklist for compliance-adjacent work, and I always ask "does this touch a process outside engineering" before finalizing an estimate. Missing that estimate taught me that most bad estimates come from missing categories of work entirely, not from misjudging the size of work I already know about.
